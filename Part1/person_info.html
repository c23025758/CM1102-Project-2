Personal Background

Alan Mathison Turing born on 23 June 1912 in Paddington, London was an English mathematician and famous computer scientist. He studied in Hazlehurst Preparatory school. He was very interested in chess and a part of the debate society.Turing did win almost every possible mathemtics prize at school. Turing used to also read Einstein's papers on relativity and quantum mechanics in Eddington's.Although Turing faced challenges during his school years, he managed to enroll at King's College, Cambridge, in 1931 to pursue a mathematics degree.



In 1933, Alan Turing got really interested in mathematical logic. He shared a paper with the Moral Science Club at Cambridge in December, and they wrote down what was discussed. This was also the year when Hitler came to power in Germany, and there was an anti-war movement in Britain. Turing joined the anti-war movement. Alan Turing also pursued studies in mathematics and cryptology at Princeton University's. Following the completion of his Ph.D. at Princeton in 1938 under the guidance of Alonzo Church, he returned to the United Kingdom. Turing then took a part-time role at the Government Code and Cypher School, a British organization dedicated to code-breaking, located at Bletchley Park. His work at Bletchley Park played a vital role in deciphering German codes during World War II.



People who knew Turing said he was a quiet and shy man. Turing was also gay. He didn't hide it, but his straightforward way of thinking made it hard for him to pretend in certain social groups. He also had a tough time making friends because he was shy. This affected his work. Sadly, in 1952, being gay was illegal. Turing got in trouble when he admitted to having a boyfriend. Instead of going to jail, he chose hormone treatment. About a year later, he died, supposedly by suicide, eating a cyanide-laced apple. The reasons behind his death are still unclear and really sad. It was truly a suicide, remain as mysterious as they are tragic.
